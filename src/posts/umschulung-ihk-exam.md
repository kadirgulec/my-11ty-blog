For seven years I worked as a specialist at the Turkish Ministry of Interior. Casework, files, deadlines. In 2016 I moved to Germany. In 2022 I started a retraining as a **Fachinformatiker für Anwendungsentwicklung**, an application developer, and in January 2025 I passed the final exam at the IHK Bonn. Today I build web applications with Laravel, Livewire and Alpine.js at aks-Service in Düren.

This post is for anyone thinking about the same step: what an Umschulung is, how the IHK exam is built, and what I would tell myself at the start.

## What an Umschulung is

In Germany, most IT jobs start with an apprenticeship (Ausbildung) that takes three years. An Umschulung is the same qualification for adults who change careers, with the same IHK exam at the end, in less time. Mine took two years, from 2022 to 2024.

It ran in two places at once:

- **EVB Training** taught the theory.
- **aks-Service** was my training company, where I worked on real projects from day one.

That combination was the most valuable part. In class you learn why something works. In the company you learn what happens when it doesn't.

## How the IHK exam works

The exam is defined by the training regulation for IT specialists ([FIAusbV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fiausbv/)). It has two parts.

**Part 1** comes in the middle of the training: a 90-minute written exam on setting up an IT workplace. It counts for 20 percent of the final result.

**Part 2** comes at the end and has four areas. For application developers they are:

| Area | Format |
|---|---|
| Planning and carrying out a software project | Company project, documentation, presentation and technical interview |
| Planning a software product | Written, 90 minutes |
| Developing and implementing algorithms | Written, 90 minutes |
| Economics and social studies | Written, 60 minutes |

The company project is the big one: it counts for half of the final result, more than the three written exams of Part 2 together. You write a project proposal first, and the IHK has to approve it before you start. Then you have **at most 80 hours** for the project and its documentation together. At the end you present it in **at most 15 minutes** and answer the examiners' questions in a technical interview, the Fachgespräch. Presentation and interview together take at most 30 minutes.

## What I would tell myself at the start

- **The code is only part of the grade.** The presentation and the Fachgespräch count as much as the project with its documentation. Plan time for writing and for practising, not only for programming.
- **The 80 hours include the documentation.** It is easy to spend 75 of them coding. Decide early how many hours go into the write-up, and keep a short log every day. It becomes the backbone of the documentation.
- **Pick a project you can explain.** In the Fachgespräch the examiners ask why you chose a solution, not only how it works. A small project with clear decisions is easier to defend than a big one you only half understand.
- **Practise the presentation out loud, with a timer.** Fifteen minutes go by fast, and the clock does not care about your last slide.
- **There is a second chance.** If one written area of Part 2 goes badly, you can ask for a short oral supplementary exam (mündliche Ergänzungsprüfung) in that area.

## Looking back

My years in public administration were not wasted. They taught me to document what I do, to work carefully and to stay calm with deadlines. All of that helps every day as a developer. The Umschulung gave me the technical side, and the company gave me the practice.

If you are thinking about a career change into IT: it is possible, also later in life and in a second language. It is a lot of work, and it is worth it.
